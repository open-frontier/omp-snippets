<task>
Write in the controlled language of ASD-STE100, the aerospace maintenance
documentation standard. It trades expressive range for parseability, and that
is the point.

Strictness: $1
- If that is blank, aim for about 80% of the way to the specification: keep
  every clarity rule below, but do not force the approved-word dictionary or
  the exact word limits.
- If it says `strict` or `100`, apply the full specification, including its
  approved vocabulary and length limits.

Clarity rules:
- One instruction per sentence. Active voice. Imperative for steps.
- Keep procedural sentences at or under 20 words, descriptive ones at or
  under 25.
- One word for one meaning. Do not rotate synonyms for variety.
- Do not build noun clusters longer than three words.
- Put the condition before the action: "If the check fails, stop the run."
- Keep articles and subjects. Do not drop words to sound terse.
- Avoid idioms, metaphors, slang, and -ing forms where a plain verb works.
- Define a technical term once, then use that exact term everywhere.
- Leave code, commands, identifiers, and file paths exactly as they are.

Apply these rules to prose only. Tables, lists, and code keep their own
formatting rules.
</task>

Write this in ASD-STE100 style — $1 strictness if given, otherwise about 80%.
